How to read this chart

The solid line is the daily median — the middle quoted price across every provider that day. Half of all offers sit above it, half below.

The shaded band spans the interquartile range: the 25th to 75th percentile. It ignores the wildest quotes on both ends and shows where the middle half of the market actually sits.

Densely traded SKUs like the H100 SXM show tighter bands than thinly quoted ones — with few offers, a single unusual listing can stretch the band dramatically.

How the deviation is computed: for every (day, SKU) pair with at least three offers, each provider's median quote is compared to the all-provider median. The figure shown is the median of those daily deviations across the provider's full history — a signed percentage above or below market.

01Premise

Basis is a variance-accounting study, not a price index. The pipeline is deliberately simple: collect quoted prices, normalize them into a canonical schema, and decompose the observed variance into observable factors versus a residual. The residual is the finding.

If a benchmark designer can't tell you which adjustments produced the headline number, the headline number is doing the wrong job.

02Data collection

Prices are collected twice daily from four source APIs — Vast.ai REST, RunPod GraphQL, AWS Spot price history, and TensorDock REST. Raw responses are stored immutably, forever: every derived number can be traced back to an original payload.

03Canonical schema

Normalization is conservative and rule-based. A reported GPU string either matches a known rule and maps to one of 96 canonical SKUs, or it is skipped and logged — never guessed. Missing values remain an explicit UNKNOWN group rather than being imputed; missing data is a category, not a gap.

04Dispersion metrics

Spread is summarized by median and interquartile range, market-wide and per provider. A (day, SKU) pair enters the analysis only with at least three offers — below that, a single listing can masquerade as a market. Thin samples are excluded rather than smoothed.

05Basis decomposition

Sequential ANOVA, in fixed model order. Each factor is credited with the variance it explains after the factors before it.

Order dependence. Reordering the factors redistributes credit among them — but never changes the residual. What the four factors jointly explain is fixed; only the story of who gets credit shifts.

Why log prices. A $0.10 gap means something different at $0.30/hr than at $6/hr. Ratios, not absolute differences, are the meaningful fungibility metric — so the model works in log space.

07What the residual means

The residual is the basis risk a compute benchmark would have to absorb. Roughly 60% of log-price variance is unexplained with marketplace listings included — around 82% with them excluded. Either way, most of the price of a GPU-hour is not accounted for by where it is, how it's committed, who sells it, or what's bundled with it.
