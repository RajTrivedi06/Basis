01 · The puzzle

A GPU-hour should be a commodity.

A GPU-hour is one hour of computing time on a graphics card — the machines that train and run AI. Companies rent them by the hour, the way you'd rent a car.

Wheat is wheat. Oil is oil. An H100 is an H100 — the hardware is literally identical. So identical things should cost roughly the same. They don't. Not even close.

03 · Cleaning up

Every cloud describes the same chip differently.

Before prices can be compared, the messy names have to be translated into one shared vocabulary. Basis does this with strict rules — and when a name doesn't match a rule, it's set aside, never guessed.

When information is missing, it's labeled UNKNOWN and kept visible. Honesty about gaps is part of the method.

06 · Why it matters

You can't build financial plumbing on a price you can't explain.

There's growing interest in treating AI compute like a commodity — with price indexes, futures, and contracts built on top of it. All of that assumes a GPU-hour has a knowable market price.

Basis suggests that assumption deserves scrutiny: most of the price of a GPU-hour is not accounted for by where it is, how it's rented, who sells it, or what comes with it. That unexplained remainder is the risk any benchmark would silently absorb.
